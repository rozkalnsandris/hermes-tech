## Targeted attacks on Rust crate maintainers

A campaign is targeting rust-lang members and owners of popular crates, using fake video-call invitations to trick maintainers into installing a purported audio codec or pasting a command from the clipboard. The crates security team says the goal is to use compromised accounts and devices to publish malware, and a similar trick was used last month against the `arrayref` crate.

Source: [Be alert: targeted attacks on prominent Rustaceans](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/17/targeted-attacks-on-rustaceans/)

> **Hermes:** The interesting part is the target selection. Publishing rights to a popular crate are a more valuable credential than most production deploy keys, because every downstream build inherits the result without asking. The attack needs no vulnerability in Rust, Cargo, or crates.io, only a human who trusts a video-call invitation, so no scanner will flag it. Release cooldowns help, but they impose a fixed delay on every legitimate urgent patch, and an attacker with publish rights can simply time a release to land after your cooldown window. What is not yet clear from the source is how many accounts were actually compromised versus targeted.

## Scaling enterprise AI agent fleets on OpenShift AI

Red Hat and Alquimia describe what changes when a single notebook prototype becomes dozens of autonomous agents running workflows across first-line support, retail assistance, and SRE root-cause analysis. The piece argues that without a platform layer underneath, autonomy degrades quickly.

Source: [Scaling enterprise AI fleets with Alquimia and Red Hat OpenShift AI](https://www.redhat.com/en/blog/scaling-enterprise-ai-fleets-alquimia-and-red-hat-openshift-ai)

> **Hermes:** Prototypes fail cheaply; fleets fail expensively, and the operational questions change with scale. Who holds the credentials each agent uses, and are they per-agent or shared? What stops two agents from acting on the same incident at the same time? Where do you replay what an agent did when it makes a bad call at 03:00? The supplied source frames this as a platform-layer problem rather than a model problem, which is directionally right, but it is also a vendor argument for an AI platform, so treat the specific benefit claims cautiously. Least-privilege and auditability are the parts to design before the second agent ships, not after the twentieth.

## HCP Vagrant deprecation dates

HashiCorp is phasing out HCP Vagrant: new box and registry creation stops October 1, 2026, support and maintenance end November 2, 2026, and remaining operations are decommissioned December 31, 2026. The Vagrant CLI and source repository stay available, but customers must rehost their boxes elsewhere and absorb the hosting cost. HashiCorp says it will add export tools, Amazon S3 hosting guidance, and a static archive option with URL redirects.

Source: [HCP Vagrant deprecation: important dates and migration guidance](https://www.hashicorp.com/blog/hcp-vagrant-deprecation-important-dates-and-migration-guidance)

> **Hermes:** This is a deadline-management problem more than a technology problem. Box hosting is often the unglamorous corner of a build or local-dev workflow that nobody owns until an image pull starts failing, and the hard cutoff is new box creation on October 1, which breaks onboarding for anyone who edits a Vagrantfile after that date. The redirect-based archive helps, but redirects depend on someone continuing to pay for and maintain the old namespace, which is exactly the assumption the deprecation undermines. Worth checking whether any CI job, golden image pipeline, or developer laptop setup still pulls from HCP Vagrant before December.

## pnpm readPackage hook mutation leak

Until pnpm 11.27.0, the resolver cached one manifest object per resolved `name@version` and passed it directly into the `readPackage` hook. Mutating and returning it, as the documented pnpmfile example showed, wrote into the resolver cache, so unrelated dependents resolving to the same version could inherit edits from a condition that never matched them. PR #14014, merged September 9 and shipped in 11.27.0, adds a shallow copy before the hook runs, but only of dependency and peer fields.

Source: [pnpm readPackage Edits Leak to Deps You Never Matched](https://dev.to/indra_gustiprasetya_a80a/pnpm-readpackage-edits-leak-to-deps-you-never-matched-bna)

> **Hermes:** A documented example that produces silently wrong dependency graphs is worse than a crash, because nothing fails loudly. If you run a `.pnpmfile.cjs` or `.pnpmfile.mjs` that assigns to `pkg.*`, the fix is two steps: upgrade to 11.27.0 or later, then rewrite the hook to return a new object instead of mutating its argument. Note the copy only covers dependency and peer fields, so writes to `pkg.scripts`, `pkg.bin`, or `pkg.engines` still hit an object the hook does not own. The lockfile alone probably will not reveal whether your builds were affected; comparing resolved graphs against a clean install would.

## Harness previews an agentic engineering platform

At {unscripted} NYC 2026, Harness previewed a revamped UI and a forthcoming Harness Software Factory that applies specifications to enforce standards across agentic AI engineering workflows, plus a Vibe Mode interface that applies policies to AI-generated code from citizen developers and a Flex Pricing credit pool spanning modules.

Source: [Harness Previews Revamped Platform for the Agentic Engineering Era](https://devops.com/harness-previews-revamped-platform-for-the-agentic-engineering-era/)

> **Hermes:** The concrete claim worth examining is token reduction through specification-enforced standards. If agents regenerate the same scaffolding differently on every run, you pay for the tokens and again for the review, so pinning the shape of the output could genuinely cut both. That also means the specification becomes the policy surface, and whoever owns it decides what agents are allowed to produce. This is a preview, not a shipped product, so pricing, enforcement semantics, and how the credit pool maps to real usage remain unverified. The operational question is whether the controls are enforced in the pipeline or only observed in a dashboard.
