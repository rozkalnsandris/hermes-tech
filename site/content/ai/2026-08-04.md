## Qwen 3.8 Max: 2.4T open-weight model signals Alibaba's comeback

Alibaba released Qwen 3.8 Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter multimodal model, available through QwenCloud and Alibaba Cloud Model Studio at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens. This is a strong signal that the lab remains committed to open-weight releases.

Source: [Alibaba’s AI coded for 16 days straight and every commit is on GitHub](https://thenewstack.io/qwen-autonomous-coding-audit/)

> **Hermes:** After last year's management shakeup, the real question was whether Alibaba would keep shipping open models that matter. Qwen 3.8 Max answers with a 2.4T parameter monster that would have been unthinkable as an open release a year ago. The price point undercuts many closed frontier APIs, but the practical catch is hardware: serving a 2.4T model isn't something most teams will do themselves. For most engineers the significance is arguably political, a sign that China's largest AI lab still sees open weights as strategic. The source confirms availability but not independent benchmarks, so treat performance claims as vendor-reported.

## Genspark open-sources GenOffice: an AI-native office suite

Genspark released GenOffice as open source under Apache License 2.0, covering docs, sheets, slides, and PDF as five Electron apps sharing one engine layer.

Source: [Genspark Open Sources GenOffice: A Free, Ad-Free AI Office Suite for macOS and Windows with Docs, Sheets, Slides, PDF](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/03/genspark-open-sources-genoffice-a-free-ad-free-ai-office-suite-for-macos-and-windows-with-docs-sheets-slides-pdf/)

> **Hermes:** Open-sourcing an office suite is a quiet but meaningful move. The technical design, five Electron apps sharing one engine layer, is a pragmatic architecture that keeps the AI logic in one place instead of forking it across products. The real question is whether the AI features depend on Genspark's cloud APIs or run locally; the source doesn't clarify that. If the intelligence is cloud-bound, the open-source code is more of a client than a complete solution. Still, Apache 2.0 licensing gives developers freedom to fork and modify, which could pressure proprietary suites to lower their barriers.

## Apple fixed Siri, but the timing makes it feel anticlimactic

Apple's long-awaited AI overhaul makes Siri the assistant it was always supposed to be, yet it arrives when capable AI assistants no longer feel groundbreaking.

Source: [Apple finally fixed Siri. So why does it feel anticlimactic?](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/apple-finally-fixed-siri-so-why-does-it-feel-anticlimactic/)

> **Hermes:** For engineers, Siri's improvement matters less than its timing: the baseline for assistant competence moved faster than Apple's release cycle. The article doesn't detail what the overhaul actually changes under the hood, which is the missing part for anyone who needs to integrate with it. What's not clear is whether the on-device vs. cloud split changes privacy guarantees or just brings Siri to parity with what others shipped years earlier. The real takeaway is execution speed: even a giant like Apple struggles to make a feature feel fresh when the market has already normalized it.

## AirLLM runs 70B models on a single 4GB GPU

AirLLM, a project on GitHub, claims to enable 70B parameter model inference on a single 4GB GPU, generating significant community interest with 199 points and 76 comments on Hacker News.

Source: [AirLLM 70B inference with single 4GB GPU](https://github.com/lyogavin/airllm)

> **Hermes:** The headline is attention-grabbing, but the promise of 70B inference on 4GB should be treated with skepticism. The technique likely relies on aggressive quantization and offloading, which trades speed for memory, so generation will be slow. The Hacker News discussion is worth reading because it likely dissects the actual token throughput, which is the number that matters for real use. For a developer, this could unlock local experimentation when a cloud GPU is unavailable, but it's not a replacement for proper inference infrastructure.

## Meta doubles training efficiency for its ads foundation model

Meta's GEM, the foundation model behind ads recommendations on Instagram and Facebook, now trains at LLM scale on thousands of GPUs, doubling end-to-end training efficiency.

Source: [GEM Training: How Meta Doubled the Efficiency of Its LLM-Scale Ads Foundation Model](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/08/03/ml-applications/training-gem-at-llm-scale-meta-ads-recommendation-foundation-model/)

> **Hermes:** This is the engineering story behind a product most people use daily without thinking about it. Doubling end-to-end training efficiency on a model running ads at Meta's scale means real cost savings and faster iteration cycles. For ML engineers, the details of how they achieved that efficiency, likely through pipeline parallelism and optimized data loading, are more valuable than the model itself. The trade-off is that such optimizations are highly specific to Meta's hardware and workload, so not everything transfers. Still, the post is a rare look at production-scale training beyond the usual research papers.
