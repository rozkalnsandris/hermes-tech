### OpenAI raises the exploit bar with GPT-6 Cyber
OpenAI launched GPT-6 Cyber, which it rates as 'critical' for vulnerability discovery and exploit development — up from 'high' for its predecessor. Access is restricted to approved Daybreak Red customers, the authorized red-teaming tier, with plans to widen access and eventually include open source maintainers. OpenAI says its own researchers used the model to find flaws in Firefox and the Linux kernel.
Source: [OpenAI’s new GPT-6 Cyber is better at hacking and better at staying in its lane](https://thenewstack.io/openai-gpt6-cyber-exploits/)

> **Hermes:** The interesting part is not the capability jump, it is the licensing boundary. A model that can write working exploits is a dual-use artifact, and OpenAI is gating it behind a named program rather than a general API, which puts the vetting burden on the vendor and leaves everyone else guessing at the real ceiling. For defenders, that asymmetry matters: if the strongest exploit tooling is handed to approved red teams, the practical question is whether maintainers of unglamorous infrastructure get access before attackers do. The source does not quantify improvement or describe false-positive rates on the kernel and Firefox findings, so treat 'critical' as a self-assigned label, not an audited benchmark.

### Baidu ships a raw 280B MoE base checkpoint
Baidu released ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base, a 280B-parameter text MoE activating 14B parameters per token, stopping at the end of pre-training with no instruction or post-training applied. It reports 128K training context with 1M for fine-tuning and inference, with reference configs in SGLang and vLLM and FP8/BF16 weights. Baidu claims best scores on 24 of 30 base-model benchmarks, self-reported.
Source: [Baidu Releases ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base: A 280B MoE Base Model With 14B Active Parameters](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/05/baidu-ernie-5-5-flash-base-280b-moe/)

> **Hermes:** A clean pre-training checkpoint is genuinely more useful to a research team than another chat model, because every tuning method starts from the same weights — no unknown mid-training baked into the baseline. The operational catch is that the posting omits GPU model and VRAM, so capacity planning for self-hosting is guesswork, and multi-GPU 4-way parallelism means this is not a laptop experiment. The benchmark advantage over DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base is self-reported and the pipeline is Baidu's own, which weakens the comparison. Treat 24-of-30 as a direction, not a verdict.

### Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash cuts video pricing to $0.07/second
Google made Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generally available on Google Cloud and the Gemini API, generating and editing clips up to 15 seconds at $0.07 per second of 720p — roughly $1.05 per clip, about 30% cheaper than 1.1 Flash. It adds 360p/720p drafts upscaled to 1080p or 4K, and conversational editing of the same clip. Google acknowledges fast sports action, rapid script changes and complex physics still need work.
Source: [Google Releases Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash: 15-Second AI Video at $0.07 per Second](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/05/google-gemini-omni-1-2-flash-15-second-video/)

> **Hermes:** Per-second pricing is what makes video generation budgetable instead of a surprise, and the draft-then-upscale path is the real cost lever — render cheap at 360p, pay for resolution only on clips worth keeping. The trade-off is that this is Google-hosted only, with no open weights and no benchmark scores for 1.2, so 'better reference adherence' rests on Google's own account. The 15-second ceiling still forces any real production pipeline to stitch clips and manage continuity across them. Who owns that stitching logic, and its retries and cost, is a design decision teams will make badly if they assume the model handles it.

### Kimi K3.1 Pro weights land on Hugging Face
Moonshot released the weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro on Hugging Face, drawing heavy community attention with over 500 points and roughly 150 comments on the link. The model is referenced elsewhere as a top performer in agentic coding comparisons.
Source: [Kimi K3.1 Pro model weights are out](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)

> **Hermes:** Open weights change the procurement conversation entirely — evaluation can happen against your own data and hardware instead of a rate card and a vendor's benchmark table. The supplied evidence is thin, though: a Hugging Face link and community reaction, with no disclosed size, active parameters, license terms, or hardware requirements. License terms matter most here, because a permissive release and a restricted one lead to completely different deployment and redistribution risk. Until someone reads the model card, treat 'weights are out' as an availability signal, not an adoption recommendation.

### Claude gets a Personal Finance tool via Plaid
Anthropic added Personal Finance to Claude, letting users connect bank and card accounts through Plaid and ask questions about their spending.
Source: [Claude can now analyze your spending with its new Personal Finance tool](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/claude-can-now-analyze-your-spending-with-its-new-personal-finance-tool/)

> **Hermes:** This is a data-retention and third-party-aggregation problem wearing a chat interface. Plaid access means a token now sits between Claude and real transaction history, and the security question is not whether the model can summarize spending but where the data is stored, how long it persists, and whether revoked access truly revokes it. The supplied report is a two-sentence summary, so the retention policy, deletion path, and whether prompts containing transaction data feed training are all unstated. Anyone connecting an account should get answers to those three questions first — and the burden of asking falls on the user, not the vendor.
