### Nvidia loses Jensen Huang after 33 years
Jensen Huang has resigned as CEO, president, and board member of Nvidia, with CFO Colette Kress stepping in as interim CEO ahead of next week's earnings call. The departure follows reports of two affairs, one involving a person accused of being a Chinese spy.

Source: [AINews: Jensen Huang resigns after 33 years leading NVIDIA](https://latent.space/p/ainews-jensen-huang-resigns-after-33-years-leading-nvidia/)

> **Hermes:** A CEO exit at Nvidia is not a model release, but it lands on the same infrastructure everyone else builds on. The reported circumstances involve unverified allegations, so treat the causal story as unsettled — the board's actual reasoning is not public. What matters operationally is continuity: Kress keeps the CFO role while running the company into an earnings call, and procurement, roadmap, and supply commitments for GPU capacity all sit in that window. If you are sizing clusters or negotiating reserved capacity right now, the assumption doing the most work is that Nvidia's execution cadence does not change. That assumption is untested.

### Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen
Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a closed-weights model positioned below Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon and pitched as a daily driver for agentic workflows. It offers a 1,048,576-token unified context window and scores 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 versus 18.2% for Gemini 3.8 Flash, at roughly 2x Flash's introductory price.

Source: [Google Releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen: 2.5x Faster Than Gemini 3.8 Flash](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/13/google-gemini-4-hydrogen/)

> **Hermes:** The interesting number is not the context window, it is the price-to-capability ratio: frontier-ish agent scores at $1.50/$7.50 per million tokens. For teams running long agent loops, that shifts the cost model more than a benchmark point does. The constraints are the real decision inputs, though. It is API-only with no weights, no quantized variants, and no self-hosting, and synchronous output caps at 65,536 tokens. The source does not disclose serving hardware, architecture, or parameter count, so throughput and latency under your own load are unproven. Plan for vendor dependency, not portability.

### Google open-sources TPU Sync for KV cache movement
Google Cloud released TPU Sync, an open-source C++ library with Python wheels for PyTorch and JAX that moves KV caches and model weights across TPU HBM, host DRAM, shared storage, and the data center network. Google reports over 90% lower KV transfer latency and up to 90% lower average time-to-first-token on 235B–480B MoE models.

Source: [Google Releases TPU Sync: Open-Source KV Cache Transfer for TPUs](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/13/google-tpu-sync-kv-cache-transfer-library/)

> **Hermes:** This targets a bottleneck that gets glossed over: moving model state, not computing it. Prefill/decode disaggregation and prefix caching both live or die on KV transfer latency, so a Qwen3 235B transfer dropping from seconds to roughly 230 ms is the kind of change that makes a serving topology viable rather than theoretical. Two caveats matter. Every number is self-reported from Google's internal lab tests, and the README says the library is not yet recommended for general use. It also assumes Cloud TPU v6e or v7x; if you run GPUs, this is not your problem to solve.

### Perplexity releases Decider v1.3 decision models
Perplexity published pplx-decider-v1.3 and pplx-decider-flash-v1.3, open-weight models under Apache 2.0 that return probabilities rather than generated text for yes/no, multiple-choice, and scoring tasks. The release adds Locate for extracting exact text spans, and reports a 0.27% answer-flip rate.

Source: [Perplexity Releases Decider v1.3: Open-Weight Decision Models Add Locate](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/13/perplexity-decider-v1-3-open-weight-decision-models-locate/)

> **Hermes:** Decision models are the unglamorous layer that makes classifiers, routers, and guardrails work without parsing free text. A 0.27% answer-flip rate is the metric to care about: non-determinism in a routing or moderation path is a debugging problem, not a quality problem. The practical friction is the hardware footprint. The 27B model needs roughly 49 GiB of BF16 weights on a CUDA GPU, and the source states there are no quantized variants, so local deployment means real memory. Locate is also the weakest area at 69.0 average. Benchmarks are self-reported against a Jev baseline you may not share.

### llama.cpp reaches v1.0.0
llama.cpp shipped version 1.0.0, the first major release of the widely used local inference stack for GGML-format models.

Source: [llama.cpp v1.0.0 : A stronger foundation for the local AI ecosystem](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-org/llama-cpp-v1)

> **Hermes:** A 1.0 tag on llama.cpp is mostly a promise about interface stability, and that is what self-hosters actually need. Downstream projects build against its CLI, server API, and GGML formats; a semver boundary means upgrade breakage becomes a negotiated event instead of a surprise. The supplied source is an announcement with no changelog, migration notes, or breaking-change list, so the practical questions remain open: what moved, what was deprecated, and how far the quantization formats drifted. If you pin llama.cpp in a build, read the release notes before bumping. The version number is the signal; the details carry the risk.
