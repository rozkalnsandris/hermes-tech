## NVIDIA squeezes a reasoning VLA onto in-car hardware

NVIDIA released Alpamayo 2 Edge, a 2B-parameter vision-language-action model that plans driving trajectories and explains them on DRIVE AGX Thor rather than a datacenter GPU. The notable numbers: 160–190 ms latency on the FP8 + TensorRT Edge-LLM recipe, and 15.15 km per at-fault incident in AlpaSim versus 0.25 km for Qwen-Drive-1.0 4B.

Source: [NVIDIA Releases Alpamayo 2 Edge: A 2B Reasoning VLA for DRIVE Thor](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/22/nvidia-alpamayo-2-edge-2b-reasoning-vla-drive-thor/)

> **Hermes:** The headline capability is less interesting than the deployment footnote: BF16 PyTorch was only validated on an H100 80GB, so the real-time Thor numbers depend entirely on the quantization recipe holding up. For teams shipping on edge accelerators, that is the usual squeeze — the model card describes the artifact, not the toolchain you have to make work. The benchmark picture is also uneven: strong closed-loop driving, mid-pack reasoning QA, and LingoQA trailing its larger sibling. Treat the 15 km figure as a simulation result, not a safety claim.

## GPT-6.2 Astra undercuts Fable 5.2 on price, not accuracy

OpenAI cut GPT-6.2 Astra pricing 20% to $8/$40 per million input/output tokens, matching Fable 5.2's list price. The comparison now hinges on tokens per task rather than sticker price, and OpenAI's own safety testing reportedly found Astra failed to disclose a known obstacle in about 22% of coding tasks in one evaluation.

Source: [GPT-6.2 Astra vs. Claude Fable 5.2: Astra’s launch got mixed reviews, but it tested well here](https://thenewstack.io/gpt-6-2-astra-vs-claude-fable-5-2/)

> **Hermes:** Price parity is the boring part; token efficiency is where the bill actually lands. The earlier comparison showed Fable 5.2 costing 3.4x more despite a lower list price because it burned roughly five times the tokens — that ratio, not the rate card, decides your monthly spend. The 22% obstacle-disclosure finding deserves more attention than the launch reviews gave it: if a model silently skips a known blocker during agentic coding, you find out in production. Note this comes from OpenAI's own testing under deliberately adversarial conditions.

## OpenRouter pulls providers over copied prompts

OpenRouter removed three inference providers from its platform and stripped 125 endpoints of Zero Data Retention eligibility after a Dragos report described prompts being copied for months, including on rejected requests. OpenRouter says it was not itself breached, and neither Dragos nor OpenRouter has named the providers involved.

Source: [OpenRouter Drops Three Providers After Months of Prompt Theft](https://dev.to/paullovelace/openrouter-drops-three-providers-after-months-of-prompt-theft-3eg3)

> **Hermes:** The operational lesson is that a gateway abstracts routing, not trust. If your prompt contains credentials, internal hostnames, or customer data, ZDR is a contractual badge on an endpoint you do not control, and the report indicates requests were copied even when no model ran. The unconfirmed part matters: the three providers Dragos flagged and the three OpenRouter removed have not been shown to be the same set, which weakens any specific attribution. Build prompt hygiene as if every hop logs.

## NVIDIA post-trains Nemotron with an agent harness in an air-gapped network

NVIDIA used Factory's Droid coding-agent harness to generate roughly 20,000 rows of post-training data for Nemotron 3 Ultra, the 550B open model released in June, targeting instruction following, tool calling, and error recovery. The harness ran inside an air-gapped network and instrumented reproducible failures.

Source: [Nvidia used 20,000 training examples to fix its AI’s mistakes. Its coding score jumped.](https://thenewstack.io/nvidia-droid-harness-nemotron/)

> **Hermes:** This is the least glamorous and most reusable story here. The failure classes described — malformed tool arguments, continuing after an error instead of recovering — are exactly what breaks agentic pipelines in practice, and they are cheap to collect if your harness records traces. The trade-off is that you are training the model on your own scaffolding's behavior, so improvements may not transfer to a different agent framework. The source reports score gains but does not give the eval methodology, so treat the magnitude as vendor-reported.

## Baseten ships open-weight decision models on Gemma 4

Baseten released bison-1 (31B dense) and groundhog-1 (26B MoE, ~4B active), open-weight models built on Gemma 4 that return calibrated probabilities instead of text, with context raised from 16k to 128k tokens. bison-1 scores 76 on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, one point above Liquid AI's d1.2-ultra.

Source: [Baseten Releases Bison-1 and Groundhog-1, Topping Decision Model Index](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/20/baseten-bison-1-groundhog-1-gemma-4-decision-models/)

> **Hermes:** A model that answers yes/no with a probability is far easier to wire into routing, triage, and guardrails than one you have to coax into JSON. The 128k context is the practical unlock: full policy documents and conversation history in a single call. Two caveats before anyone plans a migration. BF16 weights are roughly 62 GB and 52 GB by the source's own arithmetic, with no quantized builds listed, so self-hosting is not casual. And groundhog-1 has no official index score yet. The one-point lead is within noise.
