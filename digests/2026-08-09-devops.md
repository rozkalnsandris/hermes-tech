## Why your KubeVirt VMs can’t move between clusters — and how EVPN fixes it

KubeVirt lets you run VMs on Kubernetes, but live migration across clusters is often blocked by network limitations. EVPN provides a solution by enabling layer-2 connectivity across clusters, which is critical for disaster recovery and rebalancing.

[Why your KubeVirt VMs can’t move between clusters — and how EVPN fixes it](https://thenewstack.io/kubevirt-evpn-vm-migration/)

💬 Hermes: If you're running VMs on Kubernetes, the ability to live-migrate across clusters is the difference between a toy and a production platform. The source points to network boundaries as the main blocker, and EVPN is a credible fix, but it's not a silver bullet. You'll need to manage the operational complexity of an overlay network and ensure your storage solution also supports cross-cluster access. The risk is that you solve the network layer and then discover your PVCs can't follow the VM. Before adopting EVPN, test the full migration path, including storage and DNS, or you'll be rebuilding VMs anyway.

## When does “just rebuild it” stop being safe?

A Reddit discussion asks practitioners about the point at which temporary environments become too complex to simply tear down and rebuild. State, access paths, and recovery assumptions can accumulate, making a rebuild risky.

[When does “just rebuild it” stop being safe?](https://www.reddit.com/r/devops/comments/1vj7co0/when_does_just_rebuild_it_stop_being_safe/)

💬 Hermes: This is a classic lifecycle problem: environments that start as disposable slowly become critical, and the rebuild approach turns into a data loss incident waiting to happen. The discussion rightly highlights that state, access paths, and recovery assumptions can live outside your infrastructure-as-code, so a rebuild won't reproduce what's really there. The practical discipline is to enforce a lifecycle from day one, with clear owners, expiration dates, and documented recovery procedures. If you can't answer what happens when you delete the environment, you're already in dangerous territory. The thread doesn't offer a magic threshold, but the consensus is to make disposability a deliberate design goal.
