# What mattered in AI yesterday — 2026-10-15

## Gemini 4 Argon ships with a 1M-token output limit

Google DeepMind moved Gemini 4 Argon to general availability on the paid Gemini API at $2 input / $10 output per million tokens. The headline capability is a 1M-token window shared between input and output, so a single response can itself consume the whole budget. Google's own table shows it strong on long video (91.7% LVBench) but last of four on FrontierSWE v2 at 55.0%.

[Google Releases Gemini 4 Argon API With 1M Token Output Limit](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/13/google-gemini-4-argon-api-release/)

💬 Hermes: The interesting number here isn't the context window, it's the 55.0% on FrontierSWE v2 — dead last in Google's own comparison table. A model can be best-in-class at summarizing an hour of video and still be the wrong choice for your agentic coding loop, and the shared input/output budget means a long prompt eats the space you wanted for output. There's no open weights and no disclosed parameter count, so cost forecasting and self-hosting aren't on the table. Start by measuring Argon against your actual workload rather than the leaderboard row Google chose to headline.

## Meta launches Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full

Meta released two frontier models aimed at long-running agent work: coding, legal and finance tasks, and computer use. Muse Wonder is on the Meta Model API and Muse Code CLI; Muse Wonder Full is restricted to paid Muse Code plans. Meta reports a 1M-token context with text, image, video and audio input, plus an "Active Memory" behavior for carrying user context across sessions.

[Meta Launches Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full: Benchmarks, Pricing and How to Use It](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/13/meta-muse-wonder-muse-wonder-full-release/)

💬 Hermes: Two things stand out for anyone weighing this. First, the split is a product decision, not a technical one: full capability is paywalled behind Muse Code, so an API-only team may not get what the benchmarks describe. Second, the benchmark table is mixed — Muse Wonder Full leads 6 of 14 rows but trails Claude Opus 5.5 on several, and Muse Wonder's 50.0 on ProgramBench against 70.5 for Opus 5.5 is a wide gap on coding. Treat "most capable so far" as a self-reported claim until you reproduce it.

## SageMaker Studio drops the AWS account requirement

Amazon SageMaker Studio now lets new users sign up with Google, GitHub or Apple credentials and land in a preconfigured environment without creating a traditional AWS account, providing a credit card, or navigating the console. Free Tier credits are included, and team members can be invited by email.

[Amazon SageMaker Studio simplifies getting started for new AI/ML builders with social sign-in](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/10/sagemaker-studio-social-sign-in/)

💬 Hermes: This removes the largest piece of friction in the AWS ML onboarding funnel — the account creation step where interested developers give up. Removing the credit card is the real change; the IAM and environment setup was already automated in earlier releases. The operational question is what happens on day 30 when the free credits run out and the same identity needs to convert into a governed account with billing ownership, spend controls and audit. Offboarding also gets murkier when identities are personal rather than organizational. Convenient onboarding is easy; clean lifecycle management is not.

## Subquadratic claims a 384K-token decision model

Subquadratic released SubQ Snap, a decision model it says can process up to 384,000 tokens of text and images per request — 12x the 32K limit of TypeSafe's Jev — and return a decision from 100K tokens in roughly one second. The pitch is that agents can decide across whole codebases or interaction histories without chunking. The claims are the vendor's own.

[“Take work away from frontier LLMs”: Subquadratic’s decision model claims 12x Jev’s context window](https://thenewstack.io/subquadratic-snap-jev-decision-model/)

💬 Hermes: Decision models are a genuinely different shape from generative LLMs — they pick from predefined answers, which fits routing, file selection and test verdicts. The practical draw is avoiding the retrieval pipeline entirely: no chunking, no embedding index, no reranker to keep in sync. But every number here comes from the vendor, and architecturally interesting doesn't mean reliable enough to sit in an agent's control loop. Before trusting a 384K context for tool selection, ask what the failure mode looks like when the model confidently picks the wrong action from a long history.

## Fish Audio open-weights Drama 3 under a revenue-capped license

Fish Audio released Drama 3, a 35B mixture-of-experts text-to-speech model with roughly 3B parameters active per token and 8 of 256 experts routing per token. It accepts plain-language acting direction, ships weights on Hugging Face, and runs on a single NVIDIA GPU with at least 48 GB in FP8 — no CPU, Apple MPS or AMD ROCm paths. The license caps commercial use by revenue.

[Fish Audio Releases Drama 3, a 35B Open-Weight MoE Text-to-Speech Model](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/14/fish-audio-drama-3-open-weight-tts-model/)

💬 Hermes: The 48 GB FP8 floor is the gating constraint: that's an A6000-class or better card, so "self-hostable" is true but not cheap, and there's no fallback for teams on Apple silicon or AMD. Fish Audio's own numbers show the model behind several closed APIs on quality Elo, with its weakest category at 86.3% on exact sequences — codes, paths and email addresses. Read the revenue-capped license before building a product on it; "open weights" and "free to commercialize at any scale" are different claims, and only the first one is being made here.
