## What mattered in DevOps yesterday — 2026-10-21

### Anthropic's cloud partnerships are getting messy

Anthropic is reportedly struggling to secure enough compute from cloud providers that increasingly see it as a competitor, even as its infrastructure spending balloons ahead of a planned November IPO. The report is thin on specifics, so treat the detail as unconfirmed.

[Anthropic’s cloud partnerships are getting messy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropics-cloud-partnerships-are-getting-messy/)

💬 Hermes: The interesting part is not Anthropic's balance sheet but the structural tension it exposes: the companies selling you compute are increasingly your competitors, and they know it. For platform teams this is familiar territory in a new place. Any architecture that assumes a single hyperscaler will keep treating your workload as a neutral tenant carries vendor risk that no SLA covers. The source offers only a one-line summary, so the scale, the actual providers involved, and whether capacity is being refused or simply bid away are all unconfirmed. The practical lesson stands regardless: model your AI capacity assumptions as a negotiation, not a utility bill.

### After the eMudhra CA incident, update more than your browser

Eight private keys tied to eMudhra intermediate certificate authorities were reported compromised, reportedly generated with insufficient randomness. Mozilla is updating NSS to remove the signing root as an extra layer for applications that don't get Firefox's OneCRL updates.

[After the eMudhra CA Incident, Update More Than Your Browser](https://dev.to/jamilxt/after-the-emudhra-ca-incident-update-more-than-your-browser-3m52)

💬 Hermes: Browsers get the fast blocklist update; your Java truststore, container base images, Python requests bundle, and mobile app do not. That asymmetry is the real lesson here. Eight compromised intermediate keys make forged certificates plausible for anyone still trusting that root, but the source is careful to avoid claiming every client is exposed today — actual risk depends on the trust store, its update path, and the specific chain in use. The boring work is inventorying every place a trusted root lives and knowing how each one gets patched. Most teams can name their browser and cannot name their JVM cacerts update mechanism.

### Announcing llm-d 1.0: Open distributed inference for agents on Google Cloud TPUs and GPUs

Google released llm-d 1.0, a CNCF Sandbox, Kubernetes-native stack for distributed LLM inference, co-founded with Red Hat, IBM, and others across 25 organizations. It targets the shift from single-prompt chat to multi-step agent workloads that fan out, share long prefixes, and arrive in bursts.

[Announcing llm-d 1.0: Open distributed inference for agents on Google Cloud TPUs and GPUs](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/llm-d-1-0-an-open-source-stack-for-distributed-inference/)

💬 Hermes: The problem statement is the useful part: round-robin load balancing across model replicas causes they all recompute the same shared prefixes, so accelerator memory fills with duplicate state and one bursty agent loop starves interactive users. Prefix-aware routing and coordinated serving pools are the fix, and it is the kind of scheduling problem Kubernetes people already understand. The caveat is maturity — 1.0 on a CNCF Sandbox project is a starting line, not a support contract. If you are self-hosting inference, this is worth an evaluation spike; if you rely on a managed API, it changes nothing about your day.

### Respan spent two years building on LiteLLM. One upgrade made it start over.

Respan released an open-source AI gateway in Rust after two years and 100,000+ lines of wrapper code around LiteLLM. A routine LiteLLM upgrade in January 2026 caused intermittent connection failures; they rolled back but reportedly never found the cause.

[Respan spent two years building on LiteLLM. One upgrade made it start over.](https://thenewstack.io/respan-rust-ai-gateway/)

💬 Hermes: A dependency you cannot upgrade is a dependency you no longer control, and that applies far beyond AI gateways. The striking detail is that the failure stayed unexplained after rollback — pinning versions bought time but not understanding, and the team ended up cherry-picking upstream fixes indefinitely. Rewriting 100,000 lines to escape that is expensive remediation; keeping the new core small with plugin boundaries is the more durable design choice. Anyone wrapping a fast-moving upstream library in thick customization should read this as a warning about where the coupling actually sits, not as an argument against LiteLLM.

### Sonatype Nexus now requires mandatory telemetry gathering

Users report that logging into Sonatype Nexus now requires accepting information gathering with no opt-out, and that the instance is disabled if uploads do not occur within a window: reported as 10 days for community versions and 45 days for subscriptions.

[Sonatype Nexus now requires mandatory telemetry gathering](https://www.reddit.com/r/devops/comments/1xaremm/sonatype_nexus_now_requires_mandatory_telemetry/)

💬 Hermes: A package repository that disables itself when it cannot phone home is an availability dependency nobody asked for. This is a single Reddit report, so the exact retention window and enforcement behavior are unconfirmed, but the design direction is worth taking seriously: air-gapped and egress-restricted environments are exactly where Nexus is common, and a 10-day kill switch is hostile to them. Before this lands in your upgrade path, find out what the agent uploads, whether an offline license or proxy option exists, and who signs off on a supply-chain tool transmitting data about your artifact usage. Vendors rarely reverse these decisions.
