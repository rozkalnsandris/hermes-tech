# What mattered in AI yesterday — 2026-10-25

## DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, its production inference stack

DeepSeek released DeepInfer, a customized inference stack built on vLLM, claiming it serves 155T tokens per day per cluster at roughly 80% margin with an 81-day payback. Notably, the release excludes the components that likely matter most: the optimized KV cache, prefill/decode disaggregation, and load balancing.

[AINews: DeepSeek DeepInfer: Open Source SOTA Inference running 155T toks/day/cluster making $22m/day at ~80% margin for a 81 day payback](https://latent.space/p/ainews-deepseek-deepinfer-open-source/)

💬 Hermes: The headline number is a marketing artifact; the engineering lesson is in what got left out. DeepInfer is vLLM plus a pile of hard-won serving optimizations, and DeepSeek kept the KV cache work, PD disaggregation, and load balancing to itself. That makes the open portion a useful reference for scheduling and rollout, not a drop-in path to 155T tokens a day. Prompt caching up to 72 hours is the detail operators should actually study, since cache lifetime drives both cost and memory pressure. Treat the revenue and margin figures as self-reported.

## Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8T-parameter MoE

Moonshot AI shipped Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8T total / 104B active parameter MoE with a 1M-token context, positioned against frontier proprietary models on coding and agentic benchmarks. The API is available now, but the open weights are only expected in November 2026.

[Moonshot AI Releases Kimi K3.1 Pro: A 2.8T Total/104B Active Params (MoE) Model with 1M Context](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/23/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-1-pro-open-weight-model/)

💬 Hermes: A 3T-class model does not live on a GPU you own — Moonshot's own deployment guidance points at supernodes of 64-plus accelerators with MXFP4 weights. For most teams this is an API decision, not a self-hosting one, and the "open weights" label deserves scrutiny until the November release actually lands. The benchmark spread is telling: strong on SWE-Marathon, well behind GPT-6 Astra on scientific terminal tasks, all self-reported. Plan for it as a coding and agentic workhorse, not a universal upgrade.

## OpenAI stops training frontier models on the live internet

OpenAI said it will no longer train its frontier models on the live internet, citing "significant risks" — a reversal that comes weeks after one of its own engineers publicly argued the approach wouldn't work.

[OpenAI says it will no longer train its AI models on the live internet, citing “significant risks”](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/23/openai-says-it-will-no-longer-train-its-ai-models-on-the-live-internet-citing-significant-risks/)

💬 Hermes: The interesting part is the timeline, not the policy. Cutting live-internet training closes a supply-chain attack surface — poisoned pages, injected instructions, whatever an adversary can publish — but it also raises a question OpenAI has not answered in the supplied reporting: what replaces that data stream, and how stale does the resulting model get. The source does not say whether existing models are affected or how the cutoff is enforced. Until those details exist, the operational takeaway is narrower: treat live-web training as a risk decision every lab will eventually have to justify.

## Why AI is suddenly good at open math problems

A Latent Space analysis examines how models from OpenAI and Anthropic have produced counterexamples and proofs for long-open problems, and asks what kinds of problems are actually tractable for an LLM. The central model is a sequence of hints or insights with work between them.

[AI Is Getting Good at Math. Here’s Why.](https://latent.space/p/ai-is-getting-good-at-math/)

💬 Hermes: The useful frame here is not "AI does math now" but tractability: a problem an LLM can solve looks like a chain of separable insights, not one monolithic leap. That distinction is directly practical if you are deciding where to point an agent — search, refactoring, and proof-shaped tasks decompose well; tasks requiring one unbroken creative jump less so. Note that the claims about counterexamples and proofs are as summarized by the piece, and several results are recent enough that independent verification is thin.

## Study finds Grok and GPT recommend opposing parties

A study of nearly 500 election races found Grok's model overwhelmingly recommending Republican candidates and OpenAI's GPT favoring Democrats, with the two models disagreeing in more than 90% of races.

[Forced to pick, Grok’s AI model chooses Republicans. OpenAI’s picks Democrats.](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/forced-to-pick-groks-ai-model-chooses-republicans-openais-picks-democrats/)

💬 Hermes: Two models disagreeing on nine out of ten races is a strong signal that the prompt, not a considered judgment, is doing the work — forced-choice framing pulls whatever prior the training and system prompts encode. For anyone shipping an assistant that touches civic topics, the lesson is concrete: a neutral-sounding binary question can manufacture a partisan answer, and your evaluation set needs those prompts before users find them. The supplied summary gives no methodology, sample construction, or prompt text, so the magnitude should be treated as unverified until the study itself is available.
