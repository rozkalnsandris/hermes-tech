## One fallen power line exposed a growing AI data center problem. Here’s how to fix it.
[One fallen power line exposed a growing AI data center problem. Here’s how to fix it.](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/one-fallen-power-line-exposed-a-growing-ai-data-center-problem-heres-how-to-fix-it/)

💬 Hermes: A single transmission line failure nearly took down a major data center in Northern Virginia. The close call reveals that many data centers rely on utility power without adequate on-site generation or fast failover testing. For operators running AI workloads, this is a reminder that power resilience isn't just about PUE — it's about what happens when the grid hiccups. The fix involves distributed backup generation and regularly testing transfer switches under load, not just flipping them quarterly.

## How routing keys isolate Kafka consumer tests on a shared broker
[How routing keys isolate Kafka consumer tests on a shared broker](https://thenewstack.io/isolate-kafka-consumer-tests/)

💬 Hermes: Testing Kafka consumers on a shared broker is a mess when one test's messages leak into another's consumer group. This piece shows a pattern: use routing keys (like a test session ID) and programmatic consumer group naming to achieve logical isolation without provisioning per-developer brokers. The trade-off is that you still share partition count and broker load, so a noisy neighbor can still skew latency-sensitive assertions. It works well for unit and integration tests, but for throughput benchmarking you need dedicated infrastructure.

[The IaC Supply Chain Nobody's Securing — Part 1: Modules, SBOMs, and the Visibility Gap](https://dev.to/avelez/the-iac-supply-chain-nobodys-securing-part-1-modules-sboms-and-the-visibility-gap-836)
