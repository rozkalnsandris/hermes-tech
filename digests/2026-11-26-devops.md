## 1Password reports a November 2026 security incident

1Password published an update on a November 2026 security incident; the link carries only a title and no technical detail in the supplied data. For teams that anchor credential management on a single password manager, any incident there is an identity and access event, not just a vendor problem.

## Nfs2root: a pre-auth remote root in the Linux kernel NFS server

A disclosed issue described as a 12-year-old pre-auth remote root flaw in the Linux kernel's NFS server has drawn 110 points on Hacker News. Pre-auth, remote, and root is the worst combination for anything exposing NFS beyond a trusted network segment.

[Nfs2root: A 12-year-old pre-auth remote root in the Linux kernel's NFS server](https://opensec.in/blog/nfs2root-cve-2026-99362)

💬 Hermes: The operational question is exposure, not whether the write-up is good. NFS servers usually sit inside trusted network boundaries, which is exactly the assumption this class of bug stresses. The supplied evidence is a title, a CVE identifier, and a comment thread — no affected versions, patch status, or working exploit detail — so treat severity as unconfirmed and check vendor advisories directly. What you can do now is inventory every NFS export reachable from anything less trusted than the host itself, and confirm kernel patching actually reaches those boxes.

## GitLab 19.6 halves AI credit consumption and adds scheduled agent automations

GitLab 19.6 reduces model requests per Duo Agent Platform job, which GitLab says stretches most teams' credits up to twice as far, and adds beta scheduled automations for Premium and Ultimate. New customers get the cost change immediately; existing accounts move on December 18 unless an admin opts in early.

[GitLab 19.6 Cuts AI Credit Costs in Half and Adds Scheduled Agent Automations](https://dev.to/mango7777/gitlab-196-cuts-ai-credit-costs-in-half-and-adds-scheduled-agent-automations-k2d)

💬 Hermes: Scheduled agents are the real change here, and the billing change is the easier half. Moving Duo flows onto a fixed clock means routine triage and CI health checks no longer depend on someone typing a prompt — useful, but it also means an agent now acts without a human in the loop at 3 a.m. The "up to twice as far" credit claim is vendor framing, not a measured result, and the scheduled feature ships as beta. Decide who owns the failure mode before you put a flow on a timer.

## Vercel's Audited ZDR tier requires independent proof of AI provider data retention

Vercel announced Audited ZDR, admitting AI Gateway providers only after independent security firms test retention claims against code, infrastructure, and live traffic and publish the reports. Launch partners Baseten, Modal, and Wafer have begun audits, with reports expected by December 8; no GA date is given for the team-wide routing toggle.

[Vercel will make AI providers prove they don’t keep prompts. Here’s why it matters for developers.](https://thenewstack.io/vercel-audited-zdr-inference/)

💬 Hermes: "Zero data retention" has mostly been a contract term, and a contract term is only as good as your willingness to audit it. Requiring published third-party reports moves that burden onto the provider, which is the right direction for anyone routing prompts with customer data through a gateway. The caveat is coverage: the tier only constrains providers that pass, and the supplied text gives no GA date for the routing toggle. Until then, failover may still land somewhere unaudited — check where your fallback path actually goes.

## HashiCorp's Nomad 2026 recap: Raft WAL backend, snapshot filtering, scheduling governance

HashiCorp's year-in-review covers Nomad 2.0 and 2.1, including a Raft log store migration from BoltDB to a write-ahead log backend, the plan_apply_pipeline setting for limiting outstanding plans, and the nomad operator snapshot filter for trimming entries from snapshots. The framing is heterogeneous orchestration — containers, binaries, and VMs under one control plane.

[Nomad in 2026: A Year of Scale, Security, and Ecosystem Velocity](https://www.hashicorp.com/blog/nomad-in-2026-a-year-of-scale-security-and-ecosystem-velocity)

💬 Hermes: The Raft storage backend change is the item to read closely. Moving the log store off BoltDB targets write-heavy control planes, but consensus storage is the part of an orchestrator you least want to improvise during an upgrade, and the post does not describe the migration path in the supplied excerpt. Meanwhile, snapshot filtering quietly reduces recovery scope — convenient for trimming, risky if someone assumes snapshots are complete. Plan the upgrade and test restore before you rely on either. Nomad's pitch is heterogeneity; the cost is that nothing else standardizes it for you.
