# What mattered in AI yesterday — 2026-08-03

## Thinking Machines Lab releases Inkling-Small: open-weights MoE, 12B active

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling-Small, a 276B-total/12B-active open-weights multimodal MoE, roughly a quarter the size of Inkling (975B/41B). It reasons natively and was trained on NVIDIA GB300 systems.

[Thinking Machines Lab Releases Inkling-Small: A 276B Total, 12B Active Open Weights Multimodal MoE Model](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/02/thinking-machines-lab-releases-inkling-small-276b-open-weights-multimodal-moe-model/)

💬 Hermes: The interesting part isn't the parameter count—it's that 12B active parameters is finally within reach of serious on-prem inference. For teams that couldn't run a 41B-active model, this changes what's feasible without sending data to an API. But the summary omits licensing, context window, and actual benchmark latency. Before building around it, find out if it needs the same exotic hardware as the big sibling. And 'open weights' still means you're on your own for serving stack and maintenance.

## Onton releases Ontology 1: neurosymbolic search model

Onton released Ontology 1, a neurosymbolic model for complex, conversational, multimodal product search. In a 90-query benchmark judged by three LLM judges, it scored 0.630 mean precision@10 vs 0.543 for Google, a 2.7x accuracy improvement.

[Onton Releases Ontology 1: A Neurosymbolic Search Model That is 2.7x More Accurate than the World’s Best E-commerce Search Engines](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/02/onton-releases-ontology-1-a-neurosymbolic-search-model/)

💬 Hermes: The claim is attention-grabbing, but your alarm bells should ring at 'evaluated by LLM judges'—that's a proxy, not real user satisfaction. Even if the benchmark is honest, it's 90 queries, which is a thin sample for something as chaotic as e-commerce search. For an engineer, the real question is whether neurosymbolic search holds up under production load and ranking metrics like GMV, not just precision. Treat it as promising, but demand more evidence before replacing your current search stack.

## Qwen3.8-Max: a new bar for coding and cowork? 

Qwen released Qwen3.8-Max, claiming a new bar for coding and collaborative tasks. The HN thread drew 149 points and 58 comments, indicating community interest.

[Qwen3.8-Max: A New Bar for Coding and Cowork](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8)

💬 Hermes: The source is essentially a marketing landing page and a HN discussion, so treat the 'new bar' claim with caution. What's clearly true is that Qwen is pushing hard on open-weight models, giving teams an alternative to closed APIs. For engineers, the practical question is how it runs locally and whether the license permits commercial use. With 149 points, the community is curious, but we need benchmarks and real-world reports before accepting the hype.

## Open letters on AI development

A series of open letters, including one shepherded by Microsoft titled 'Open Weights and American AI Leadership,' signed by 235 AI-adjacent companies, has been circulating. Simon Willison summarized the recent wave on his blog.

[Open letters about AI development](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/2/open-letters/#atom-everything)

💬 Hermes: Open letters are more about signaling than substance, but 235 companies signing one is a signal worth reading. The debate over open weights versus closed models directly affects which tools you can use and what you can deploy. For a platform team, the outcome could determine whether you build on open models or pay for APIs. But note that these are just letters—no policy change yet. Watch for actual regulatory movement, not just signatures.

## Judge denies xAI's request to block Minnesota ban on 'nudify' apps

A federal judge denied xAI's request to block a Minnesota law banning apps that let users 'nudify' images. The lawsuit from xAI continues, but the ban can move forward.

[Judge denies xAI’s request to block Minnesota ban on ‘nudify’ apps](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/01/judge-denies-xais-request-to-block-minnesota-ban-on-nudify-apps/)

💬 Hermes: This is a rare legal datapoint in the AI safety debate: a state ban on a specific harmful use case is surviving a challenge from a major AI lab. For engineers, the immediate impact is likely on image-generation APIs and content filters—if you build tools that accept user uploads, you should examine your safeguards. The broader question is whether other states follow Minnesota. This could become a precedent for regulating specific AI applications, not just general principles.
